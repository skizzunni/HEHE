    One line per game — the side I have winning
    
  
    Every row is a single call. The opponent is not listed because it is not the point:
  this is who I think wins, the confidence behind it, and the numbers that got me there.
    
  
    Each league runs the method that won its own held-out backtest — MLB from starter
  ERA and FIP, bullpen ERA and team run rates; basketball, football and hockey from
  margin-of-victory ratings; tennis from a level-seeded surface Elo with an experience
  ramp. Held-out backtest accuracy and what each league has actually done since are shown
  in Record vs claim below — they differ, sometimes by a lot, and the live
  column is the one that counts.
    
  
    Tennis was rebuilt on 2026-09-03. The old ranking-points model quoted 60.2%, but that
  figure was contaminated — current rankings already contain the results of the matches
  being predicted, and ESPN serves no dated rankings table. The number above is walk-forward
  on 1,996 held-out July and August matches with every parameter fitted on April–June
  only. The gain is almost entirely one thing: a debutant was being seeded at the same rating
  as an established player at that level, and ramping newcomers up over their first 20 matches
  is worth +6.2 points of accuracy
  (95% CI +4.4 to +8.1, paired bootstrap). Expect less than that in practice: half the
  held-out matches involve a player with under 20 prior matches, which is an artifact of a
  rating pool that starts everyone from scratch in January, and that share falls once the
  pool matures.
    
  
    Price is shown only where a book has posted one, and a
  DOG tag means I am taking the side priced as less likely.
  Those are the calls that are genuinely mine rather than an echo of the number — and
  also the ones most likely to be my error: over 655 MLB games the market called 57.3%
  against my 55.9%.
    
  
    Nothing here is a lock. On 8/30 four underdogs won of thirteen priced MLB games,
  and my most confident pick of that day lost to a +151 dog.
