What the Lean column actually is

It is the market's favourite with the bookmaker's margin stripped out — not a model pick. That is a deliberate choice, and it cost something to learn.

Measured across 655 MLB games against real closing prices, the market called 57.3% of them correctly and my model 55.9%. Worse, backing the model wherever it disagreed with the book lost money, and lost more the louder it disagreed: −1.0% at a two-point gap, −14.3% at ten. So the board shows the better forecast rather than mine.

Probabilities are de-vigged, so a 60% here means the book prices the game near 60% — the price you are actually offered already carries their margin on top. Analysis shows the probable starters and their ERAs, so you can see what is driving the number rather than taking it on faith.

Only games that have not started appear here. Finals are dropped — a settled game is not a pick.

Tomorrow's rows fill in as books post — most MLB lines go up overnight, and an event with no number simply had no price when this was captured.

Nothing here is a lock. A 14-game slate at a 57% hit rate swings between six and ten winners on an ordinary day. That range is the whole story of a good night and a bad one.
